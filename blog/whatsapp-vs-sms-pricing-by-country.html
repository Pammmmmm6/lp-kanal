WhatsApp vs SMS Pricing by Country: 20-Market Comparison (2026)

Compare WhatsApp vs SMS cost across 20 markets in 2026: indicative rates, break-even analysis for Shopify stores and how to cut your messaging bill.

Nicolas ProvostMay 12, 2026 · 20 min read

Key takeaways

  • Meta charges each delivered WhatsApp template, so a multi-message marketing flow is no longer cheaper than SMS by default.
  • WhatsApp wins on utility flows, where a US utility message costs $0.0034 against $0.0083 plus carrier fees for an SMS segment on Twilio.
  • Marketing rates run from $0.0109 in Turkey and $0.0118 in India to $0.1597 in the Netherlands, and marketing templates to US numbers are paused.

The most common mistake e-commerce teams make when comparing WhatsApp and SMS is reading the headline numbers. A WhatsApp marketing message can look expensive next to a single SMS, until you factor in the much cheaper utility category and the replies you send in the 24-hour customer service window, of which Meta does not charge the first 1,000 per business phone number each month.

In this guide we'll break down how WhatsApp and SMS pricing actually work, give you a 20-market price table, show you exactly where each channel wins on cost, and walk through a 5,000-orders-per-month example so you can see the break-even on your own Shopify store.

How WhatsApp pricing works

WhatsApp's pricing model differs from SMS in what gets billed. Since July 1, 2025, Meta charges per delivered template message. Until June 30, 2025, it charged per 24-hour conversation, a model that is now deprecated. You pay when a template is delivered, not when it is sent, and the messages customers send you are never charged.

Each template falls into one of three categories, and the category determines the rate (Meta's pricing, per delivered message):

  • Marketing: promotional content, new product launches, abandoned cart recovery. Highest rate: $0.0250 per delivered message on Meta's North America rate card, though Meta has paused marketing templates to US numbers since April 1, 2025 (see WhatsApp marketing in the USA).
  • Utility: order updates, shipping notifications, account alerts, anything tied to a specific transaction. $0.0034 per delivered US message, lower with monthly volume tiers.
  • Authentication: OTP codes, login verifications. Same rate as utility in the markets of the table below ($0.0034 in the US), also with volume tiers.

Service messages are not a template category. They are your free-form replies inside the 24-hour customer service window that each customer message opens (or resets). They were not charged until September 30, 2026. Since October 1, 2026, Meta charges them at the market's utility rate after 1,000 free service messages per business phone number each month.

The country calling code of the recipient's number sets the rate. Turkey and India are the cheapest markets in the table below. The Netherlands, Germany, and France are the most expensive for marketing messages. For the full official methodology and the message categories, see the Meta WhatsApp pricing documentation.

A typical e-commerce conversation includes:

  1. Your outbound template message (e.g. "Your order #1234 has shipped")
  2. The customer's reply ("Where is the tracking?")
  3. Your follow-up ("Here's the link: ...")
  4. Several more back-and-forth messages

Only the first message is a billable template. The customer's messages are never charged, and your free-form follow-ups are service messages: billed at the utility rate beyond 1,000 free per business phone number each month. That's the structural difference with SMS, where every leg is billed.

How SMS pricing works

SMS is priced per message, full stop. Every outbound message generates a billable event, and inbound messages are usually charged separately (or routed through a different short code). The rate depends on:

  • The destination country: operator termination fees vary widely between countries
  • The route: direct operator routes are more reliable but more expensive than grey routes
  • Volume: high-volume senders negotiate lower rates, but Shopify stores rarely qualify
  • Sender ID: alphanumeric sender IDs cost more than numeric short codes in some markets
  • Message length: anything over 160 characters splits into multiple billable segments (see what SMS means and how its length limit works)

SMS providers add their own margin on top of operator termination fees, and each publishes its own price list. For the apps themselves, see our comparison of SMS marketing apps for Shopify, and for the two channels side by side on a store, SMS marketing for Shopify.

The fundamental issue with SMS for e-commerce isn't the cost of the first message. It's the lack of a reply window. Every "we have a question about your order" exchange, every back-and-forth that closes a deal, each leg costs another full SMS.

Break-even analysis: when WhatsApp wins

Since July 1, 2025, WhatsApp no longer bundles a multi-message flow into one fee: each delivered template is charged, like each SMS. WhatsApp's cost edge now comes from three places: utility messages priced below Twilio's SMS price in every market of the table above, customer messages that are never charged, and replies inside the 24-hour customer service window, of which the first 1,000 per business phone number each month are not charged. Here are the e-commerce use cases where it matters most:

Abandoned cart recovery

A standard abandoned cart sequence sends 3-5 messages over 24-72 hours. With abandoned cart SMS, that's 3-5 billable messages per cart. With WhatsApp, it's also 3-5 billable marketing messages: Meta charges each delivered template, follow-ups within 24 hours included. If the customer replies, you get a 24-hour customer service window where you can answer with free-form messages, no template needed. Those replies were not charged until September 30, 2026. Since October 1, 2026, they cost the utility rate after 1,000 free service messages per business phone number each month.

In France, a 4-step abandoned cart flow costs 4 x $0.0798 = $0.3192 per cart on SMS at Twilio's price for France. On WhatsApp, the same 4 marketing messages cost 4 x $0.0859 = $0.3436 per cart at Meta's list rate (Meta's pricing, per delivered message). On unit cost alone, SMS is slightly cheaper for this flow in France, so the WhatsApp case rests on the replies and conversions you measure on your own store, and on sending fewer, better-timed reminders.

For a deep dive on this flow, see our guide on abandoned cart recovery strategies.

Post-purchase flow

Order confirmation, shipping update, delivery confirmation, review request. That's 4 SMS messages per order. On WhatsApp, it's 4 templates, each charged when delivered, but transactional updates run as utility templates, Meta's lower-priced category. Utility templates sent while a customer service window is open (the customer wrote to you in the last 24 hours) were not charged until September 30, 2026 and are charged again since October 1, 2026; the customer's own questions are never charged.

A US Shopify store doing 5,000 orders/month would pay $59 to $66.50 a month on SMS for one shipping update per order: 5,000 messages at Twilio's US price of $0.0083 per segment plus a carrier fee of $0.0035 to $0.005. On WhatsApp utility, it's $0.0034 x 5,000 = $17/month at Meta's US list rate (Meta's pricing, per delivered message), before any volume-tier discount.

Customer support deflection

Service messages, your free-form replies inside the 24-hour customer service window, were not charged from November 1, 2024 to September 30, 2026. The customer's own messages are never charged. If you migrate your support inbox to WhatsApp and let customers DM you their question, a support thread cost nothing in Meta fees until September 30, 2026. Since October 1, 2026, Meta charges your replies at the market's utility rate ($0.0034 per delivered US message, per Meta's pricing) after 1,000 free service messages per business phone number each month. Compare that to SMS, where you'd pay for every reply.

Check what your support replies cost on SMS today to size the saving on your own ticket volume. See the WhatsApp Business pricing guide for the message categories and the October 1, 2026 change.

Cross-border traffic

WhatsApp has a global single-API model. SMS providers charge wildly different rates per country, and the markup on premium routes is often hidden. For Shopify stores selling into Latin America, Southeast Asia, or the Gulf, WhatsApp delivers more predictable unit economics.

Break-even analysis: when SMS still wins

SMS isn't dead. There are three specific scenarios where it can still win on cost:

One-shot transactional blasts

If you're sending a single message that you know won't generate a reply (a flash sale notification, a one-time coupon, an emergency stock-out alert), and your SMS price for the recipient's country is below Meta's marketing rate, SMS can beat WhatsApp marketing: in France, for example, $0.0798 per SMS segment on Twilio against $0.0859 per delivered marketing message (Meta's pricing). That is the typical case for a one-off SMS campaign.

The catch: replies change the math. SMS replies are billed. On WhatsApp, the customer's messages are never charged, and your answers inside the 24-hour customer service window were not charged until September 30, 2026 (since October 1, 2026, they cost the utility rate after 1,000 free service messages per business phone number each month).

Markets with low WhatsApp adoption

In the US, WhatsApp is not the default messaging app, while any mobile phone can receive an SMS. If a large part of your audience does not use WhatsApp, SMS gets to more eyes per dollar spent: check how many of your customers opt in to WhatsApp before you move a flow. For promotional sends to US numbers, SMS is also the only option of the two for now: Meta has paused marketing templates to US numbers since April 1, 2025.

This is a deliverability question, not a pricing one, but it shows up on the bottom line. Meta only charges delivered messages, so a WhatsApp message that can't reach the customer costs nothing in Meta fees, but it sells nothing either, while an SMS that does reach them can.

Authentication in cheap-SMS countries

For pure OTP flows where the user doesn't continue the conversation, SMS wins wherever your SMS price is below Meta's authentication rate for the recipient's country. Check before you decide: at Meta's list rates, authentication costs the same as utility in the markets of the table above, for example $0.0014 per delivered message in India and $0.0068 in Brazil (Meta's pricing).

Tips to reduce WhatsApp messaging cost

A few playbook moves can cut your WhatsApp bill without reducing volume.

1. Route flows as utility, not marketing

Order confirmations, shipping updates, delivery notifications, and review requests all qualify as utility messages. In the UK, a utility message costs $0.0220 against $0.0635 for marketing (Meta's pricing, per delivered message), and for US numbers a template classified as marketing is not delivered at all while Meta's pause lasts. Make sure your provider classifies templates correctly when they're submitted to Meta for approval.

2. Encourage customer replies

Each customer message opens (or resets) a 24-hour customer service window. Inside it, you can answer with free-form messages instead of templates: these service messages were not charged until September 30, 2026, and since October 1, 2026 they cost the utility rate after 1,000 free per business phone number each month. End every utility message with a question that invites a reply ("Got any questions about your order?"). Each reply lets you follow up with a free-form message instead of a marketing template.

3. Combine marketing content instead of multiplying sends

Since July 1, 2025, two templates sent to the same customer within 24 hours no longer share one billable conversation: each delivered template is charged. If a weekend flash sale and a related product recommendation go to the same customer, put both in one template: one delivered marketing message, not two.

4. Use authentication templates correctly

For OTPs and login codes, use the authentication category, not marketing: in the UK, Meta charges $0.0220 per delivered authentication message against $0.0635 for marketing (Meta's pricing). Check that your BSP (Business Solution Provider) submits OTP templates as authentication: an OTP template filed as marketing is charged the marketing rate.

5. Build opt-in quality, not opt-in volume

Low-quality opt-ins (scraped lists, incentivized signups without intent) generate complaints and downgrade your messaging tier. A downgraded tier means lower daily message limits and fewer customers reached per campaign. See our WhatsApp marketing guide for opt-in best practices.

6. Pick a provider with no per-message markup

Some BSPs charge a markup on top of Meta's rates. Kanal passes WhatsApp fees through at cost, with no per-message markup, and plans start from $89/month. On a large monthly volume, even a small per-message markup adds up. Compare Kanal pricing, where a cost simulator shows your total with WhatsApp fees included, against your current provider.

How WhatsApp vs SMS stacks up against RCS

A quick sidebar on RCS (Rich Communication Services), since it's increasingly relevant for e-commerce in 2026. RCS supports rich media, and its price depends on the provider and the country: Twilio, for example, lists an RCS Basic message to the UK at $0.056, the same as an SMS segment (Twilio's UK pricing, read on October 8, 2026). It lacks the 24-hour customer service window and the global API consistency that WhatsApp offers. For the full three-way comparison, see SMS vs MMS vs RCS vs WhatsApp. For the basics, see what an RCS message is and the difference between SMS and MMS, and for the merchant side, how RCS business messaging works.

For most Shopify stores in 2026, the recommendation remains: WhatsApp as primary channel, SMS as fallback for non-WhatsApp users, RCS as an optional layer in supported markets (Kanal has a page on RCS for Shopify stores).

Conclusion

Comparing channels on the price of a single message is an easy way to overspend on messaging. Meta charges each delivered WhatsApp template, so count every template in a flow, then factor in the category (utility costs far less than marketing) and WhatsApp's 24-hour customer service window, where you can answer with free-form messages, no template needed: since October 1, 2026, the utility rate after 1,000 free service messages per business phone number each month. The right comparison is flow by flow, not message by message.

The three actions to take this week:

  1. Audit your current SMS spend by flow type, then map each flow to its WhatsApp equivalent using the table above.
  2. Check that your utility templates are classified as utility, not marketing, when they are submitted to Meta.
  3. Migrate your highest-volume flow (usually shipping updates, or abandoned cart outside the US) to WhatsApp first, measure the cost delta for 30 days, then expand.

Frequently asked questions

Is WhatsApp cheaper than SMS for marketing campaigns?

It depends on the market and on the flow. Since July 1, 2025, Meta charges each delivered marketing template, follow-ups included: in France, $0.0859 per delivered marketing message (Meta's list rate), to compare with your SMS price there. In the US, Meta has paused marketing templates to US numbers since April 1, 2025. WhatsApp's edge comes from replies: customer messages are never charged, and your answers inside the 24-hour customer service window were not charged until September 30, 2026 (since October 1, 2026, they cost the utility rate after 1,000 free service messages per business phone number each month).

How does WhatsApp's per-message pricing actually work?

Since July 1, 2025, Meta charges each delivered template message. The old per-conversation model, where one fee covered a 24-hour window, is deprecated. The template category (marketing, utility, or authentication) and the country calling code of the recipient's number set the rate. Customer messages are never charged. Service messages, your free-form replies inside the 24-hour customer service window, were not charged until September 30, 2026. Since October 1, 2026, they cost the utility rate after 1,000 free per business phone number each month.

Which countries have the cheapest WhatsApp pricing?

Among the markets in this guide, Turkey ($0.0109) and India ($0.0118) are the cheapest per delivered marketing message, followed by Canada ($0.0250) and Poland ($0.0366), per Meta's list rates read on October 7, 2026. The US has the same North America rate, but Meta has paused marketing templates to US numbers since April 1, 2025. Western Europe is generally the most expensive for marketing messages: the Netherlands ($0.1597), Germany ($0.1365), and France ($0.0859) top the table. For other countries, check Meta's rate calculator.

When does SMS still beat WhatsApp on cost?

SMS can win in three scenarios: one-shot single-message blasts where you know the customer won't reply, markets with very low SMS rates and no WhatsApp adoption (some US carrier campaigns), and authentication codes in countries where Meta's authentication rate is higher than your SMS price. Since each WhatsApp template is charged, marketing flows can also cost less on SMS in markets where a WhatsApp marketing message costs more than an SMS. Utility flows like order and shipping updates usually favor WhatsApp: $0.0034 per delivered US utility message (Meta's list rate).

How can I reduce my WhatsApp messaging bill?

Use utility templates for transactional flows ($0.0220 per delivered UK utility message against $0.0635 for marketing, Meta's list rates), encourage customers to reply (your free-form answers inside the 24-hour customer service window were not charged until September 30, 2026, and now cost the utility rate after 1,000 free service messages per business phone number each month), combine related marketing content into one template instead of several sends, and choose a provider with no per-message markup on top of Meta's rates.
