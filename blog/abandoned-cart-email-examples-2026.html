Abandoned Cart Email: 12 Examples That Convert (2026)

Abandoned cart email guide: 12 examples with full copy, subject line patterns to test, the 1h/24h/72h send sequence and how to pair it with WhatsApp.

Nicolas ProvostMay 19, 2026 · 14 min read

Key takeaways

  • Three messages over 72 hours is a sensible starting point, with the first reminder sent within 1 hour of the abandoned cart.
  • The first message should never carry a discount, because a 10% code on email one trains customers to abandon for the coupon.
  • Email costs under $0.001 per send while Meta charges $0.0859 per WhatsApp marketing message in France, so sequence both and compare recovery per channel.

About 70% of online shopping carts are abandoned before checkout completes, according to the Baymard Institute. Run the math with your own numbers: a Shopify store that completes $50,000 of orders a month with a 70% abandonment rate leaves roughly $116,000 in carts behind, if abandoned carts are the same size as completed ones. Some of those shoppers were a hairline away from buying. They just needed a nudge.

The good news: with the right messages at the right time, you can win back part of those lost carts. This guide breaks down 12 abandoned cart email templates you can adapt, the timing logic behind each, and how to pair email with WhatsApp.

Why abandoned cart emails still matter in 2026

Cart abandonment hasn't gone away. According to the Baymard Institute, the average documented cart abandonment rate is 70.22%, calculated from 50 different studies. Setting aside shoppers who were just browsing, the top reasons US shoppers give are:

  1. Extra costs too high (shipping, taxes, fees): 40%
  2. Delivery was too slow: 20%
  3. Didn't trust the site with card info: 19%
  4. Site wanted them to create an account: 18%
  5. Too long or complicated checkout: 17%

What HAS changed is the recovery toolkit. Email is still the workhorse: Mailchimp's benchmark puts the average e-commerce open rate at 29.81% (data available in December 2023). WhatsApp adds a channel shoppers read in the app they already use to chat, and our 2026 WhatsApp marketing statistics page lists the platform figures we could source. The winning playbook in 2026 isn't "email vs WhatsApp", it's both, sequenced.

12 abandoned cart email templates that convert

Template 1: The soft reminder (1-4h after abandon)

Subject: You forgot something, {first_name}

Hi {first_name},

Looks like you left a few things in your cart. No worries, they're still waiting for you.

{product_name} {price}

[Finish my order ->]

If something went wrong at checkout, just reply to this email and we'll sort it out.

The {brand} team

Why it works: Low pressure, no discount, opens the door for replies. The "reply to this email" line gives shoppers who hit a checkout error a way to tell you, so you can fix it and save the order.

Best for: Default first send. Almost all brands should use this.

Template 2: The social proof angle (24h)

Subject: Why {review_count} customers love {product_name}

{first_name},

Did you know {product_name} is one of our best-sellers?

"{customer_review_excerpt}" {customer_name} - verified buyer (5 stars)

Your cart is still saved: {product_name} - {price}

[Resume my order ->]

Free shipping over {threshold} Free returns within 30 days

{brand}

Why it works: Social proof crushes hesitation. A review from a verified buyer answers the "is it worth it?" doubt at the moment the shopper is deciding.

Best for: Stores with strong review volume (50+ reviews per SKU).

Template 3: The last chance + discount (48-72h)

Subject: Your cart expires soon (small gift inside)

{first_name},

Your cart expires in 24 hours. Before it disappears, here's a little something on us:

10% off with code {CODE}

{product_name} - {price} {discounted_price}

[Claim my discount ->]

Code valid for 24 hours only.

Why it works: Discount + deadline triggers loss aversion. The "small gift" framing softens the discount and avoids the "we expected you to leave" feeling.

Best for: Closing sequence message. Never the first one.

Template 4: The free shipping angle (alternative to %off)

Subject: Free shipping unlocked for your cart

{first_name},

Good news. We're covering shipping on your order.

{product_name} - {price} Free shipping (save {shipping_cost})

[Checkout with free shipping ->]

Offer valid 48 hours.

Why it works: Extra costs (shipping, taxes, fees) are the top abandonment reason in Baymard's survey of US shoppers. Covering shipping removes that cost without touching the product price, which protects brand price perception. Test it against a %-off code on your own store.

Best for: Beauty, apparel under $100, and stores that don't want to discount product price.

Template 5: The objection-handler (24h, mid-AOV stores)

Subject: A few things you might want to know

{first_name},

You left {product_name} in your cart. Before you decide, here's what makes most customers pull the trigger:

Made in {origin_country}, ships within 48h 30-day no-questions-asked returns Climate-positive shipping (we offset 100% of CO2) 24/7 WhatsApp support: chat with a human in seconds

[Pick up where you left off ->]

Why it works: Some buyers abandon because of doubts they never voice. Pre-empting them in a bullet list crushes friction without resorting to a discount.

Best for: $100+ AOV products where trust is the main blocker.

Template 6: The stock-pressure email (48h)

Subject: Only {stock} left of {product_name}

{first_name},

Heads up: {product_name} is almost sold out.

Only {stock_count} left in {size_or_color}.

[Grab yours before it's gone ->]

Not the one for you? Browse similar styles here.

Why it works: Genuine scarcity (real stock numbers) gives the shopper a real reason to decide now. Critical: only use real stock counts. Fake scarcity is now FTC-flagged in the US and EU-illegal under the Omnibus Directive.

Best for: Fashion, limited-edition drops, beauty.

Template 7: The complete-the-look upsell (24h)

Subject: We picked these out for {product_name}

{first_name},

Your cart is still saved. While you decide, here are a few pieces that pair beautifully with {product_name}:

[Carousel: 3 complementary products with image + price + Add to cart]

Free shipping over {threshold}.

[Back to my cart ->]

Why it works: Reframes the email from "you abandoned" to "here's value", and gives the shopper a reason to add a matching piece rather than just complete the cart.

Best for: Fashion, beauty, home goods, anything with cross-sell logic.

Template 8: The plain-text personal note (24-48h)

Subject: Quick question about your order

Hi {first_name},

Saw you left {product_name} in your cart and wanted to check in personally.

Was there anything you couldn't find on the site, or any questions about the product I can answer?

Reply to this email and I'll get back to you within the hour.

Best, {founder_first_name} Founder, {brand}

Why it works: Plain text emails from founders cut through inbox noise and invite a reply, and every reply is a conversation you can turn into an order.

Best for: Small to mid-sized DTC brands where the founder is part of the brand story.

Template 9: The educational angle (24h, considered purchases)

Subject: How to choose the right {product_category}

{first_name},

Picking a {product_category} can feel overwhelming. Here's a quick guide we put together:

  1. Match {dimension_1} to your {use_case}
  2. Look at {dimension_2}, it matters more than people think
  3. {dimension_3} is overrated. Focus on {real_dimension}

Your cart is still saved: {product_name} - {price}

[Read the full guide ->] [Or go straight to checkout ->]

Why it works: Education builds trust without pushing. It can take longer to convert, but the shopper who buys knows why they chose the product.

Best for: High-consideration purchases (mattresses, skincare regimens, tech, supplements).

Template 10: The bundle alternative (48h)

Subject: We turned your cart into a bundle (15% off)

{first_name},

Save more on what you already wanted. Bundle your items and save 15%:

Your cart: {product_1} - {price_1} {product_2} - {price_2}

Bundle price: {bundle_price} (save {savings})

[Get the bundle ->]

Valid 48 hours.

Why it works: Reframes the discount as value-add rather than price drop. Protects brand pricing.

Best for: Multi-item carts ($60+ subtotal). To create the offer itself, see our list of the best bundle apps for Shopify.

Template 11: The reactivation (7+ days, the long-tail recovery)

Subject: Come back to {brand}, {first_name}?

{first_name},

It's been a while. The {product_name} you were considering is still here, and we restocked all your favorite colors.

Here's $15 off your next order with code WELCOME15.

[Take a look ->]

No pressure. We'll be here when you're ready.

Why it works: Reactivation: one last, low-pressure attempt on long-abandoned carts that would otherwise be dead.

Best for: Day 7+ in the sequence, or quarterly re-engagement campaigns.

Template 12: The post-purchase reroute (immediately, when applicable)

Subject: We saved your cart while you ordered

{first_name},

Thanks for ordering {recent_product}! We noticed you also had {abandoned_product} in your cart.

Want to add it to your shipment? We can ship them together for free if you order in the next 30 minutes.

[Add to my order ->]

Why it works: Captures the rare case where a customer bought one item but left another behind, and it can win back these "split abandons" with zero discount cost.

Best for: Stores running post-purchase upsell flows.

Subject line patterns that work

Three subject line patterns worth A/B testing against each other:

PatternExampleWhy it works
Name + product"{first_name}, your {product} is waiting"Personal and specific: it names what they left
Question"Forget something?"Curiosity without pressure
Value-add"We saved you {amount} on shipping"Leads with a benefit, not a reminder

Three subject line patterns to avoid:

  • ALL CAPS (triggers spam filters and looks pushy)
  • "Last chance" in the first email (educates customers to wait for discount)
  • Emoji-heavy ("Your cart misses you")

Best practices for any cart recovery sequence

1. Don't drop the discount early

Lead with a clean reminder. If you offer 10% off on email #1, you train customers to abandon every time to get the coupon. The first message should NEVER have a discount.

2. Personalize everything

Emails with first name AND product name AND product image remind the shopper of exactly what they left, which a generic "you forgot something" email cannot do. Modern ESPs and Kanal pull these from Shopify automatically. No excuse not to. If you are still choosing an ESP, see our comparison of Shopify email marketing apps.

3. Respect the timing

  • WhatsApp: 1h, 24h, 72h
  • Email: 4h, 48h, 72h
  • SMS: only as a last resort, 48-72h (high cost, high opt-out risk)

4. A/B test the variables that move the needle

The variables worth testing:

  • Subject line angle (urgency vs curiosity vs benefit)
  • Incentive type (% off vs free shipping vs bundle vs gift)
  • Send time of day
  • Subject line length

Don't bother A/B testing color of buttons. The juice isn't worth the squeeze.

5. Measure the right metrics

KPIWhat it tells you
Open rate by channelSubject line / channel fit
Click rate per messageBody copy effectiveness
Conversion rate (message-attributed)True recovery impact
Total revenue recoveredThe number that pays your bills
Unsubscribe rateAre you fatiguing the list?

The full KPI framework lives in the ROI of WhatsApp marketing and KPIs post.

6. Stay compliant

Email needs CAN-SPAM and GDPR compliance, an unsubscribe link, and a physical address in the footer. WhatsApp needs explicit prior opt-in plus the GDPR-compliant opt-in flow for EU shoppers. SMS needs TCPA-compliant consent in the US.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best send time for an abandoned cart email?

The first email should go out within 1 hour of cart abandonment when purchase intent is still hot: the longer you wait, the more likely the shopper has bought elsewhere or moved on. A three-message sequence at 1h, 24h and 72h is a sensible default; test the timings against your own recovery data before adding more sends.

What makes a high-converting abandoned cart email subject line?

Good subject lines reference the product by name, include the customer's first name and create gentle urgency without being pushy. Examples include "Your [product] is still waiting", "[Name], forget something?" and "We held your cart for you". Avoid all-caps and discount-heavy lines for the first email; they educate customers to abandon for the coupon.

How many abandoned cart emails should I send?

Three messages over 72 hours is a sensible starting point for most DTC stores. Beyond three sends, you mostly add pressure on shoppers who have decided not to buy, and you start hitting spam complaints. A smart sequence mixes channels: WhatsApp at 1h, email at 4h, WhatsApp at 24h, then email + WhatsApp at 72h with a coupon as the closer.

Is WhatsApp more effective than email for cart recovery?

They do different jobs. WhatsApp reaches shoppers in the chat app they already use and lets them reply, which suits the first hours after abandonment; email is better for the longer tail. There is no independent public benchmark for WhatsApp cart recovery and vendor figures are not audited, so compare both channels on your own store with a holdout group. The catch is volume: email is cheap to send, while Meta charges each delivered WhatsApp marketing message ($0.0859 in France, $0.0635 in the UK, per Meta's pricing page), so the math wins on high-AOV stores first.

What's a good conversion rate for abandoned cart emails?

There is no independent public benchmark for abandoned cart email conversion: vendor figures are not audited and do not use the same definition. The reliable number is your own: recovered orders divided by carts that entered the flow, tracked per message and per channel, and compared against a holdout group that receives no reminder.
