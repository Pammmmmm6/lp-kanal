Shopify vs WooCommerce: Costs, Fees and Control (2026)

Shopify vs WooCommerce compared for US stores: subscription or hosting costs, payment fees, maintenance, flexibility, apps and how to migrate between them.

Nicolas ProvostOct 7, 2026 · 11 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify is a hosted platform rented by the month, while WooCommerce is an open-source WordPress plugin you host and maintain yourself.
  • Shopify Basic costs $29 a month paid yearly, against WooCommerce hosting that its own pricing page puts at $25 to $350 a month.
  • US card rates match at 2.9% + 30¢, but Shopify adds a 2% third-party transaction fee on Basic when you use another processor.

Shopify is a hosted platform that you rent by the month, and WooCommerce is a free, open-source plugin that turns a WordPress site you host yourself into a store. For a US store as of October 7, 2026, Shopify starts at $39 a month ($29 paid yearly) plus 2.9% + 30¢ per online card payment, while WooCommerce has no subscription but leaves you to pay for hosting, extensions and maintenance, with card payments at 2.9% + $0.30 through WooPayments.

Each side of this comparison comes from the company's own pages, read on October 7, 2026: Shopify's pricing page, with the prices shown to US stores, and its Help Center, and WooCommerce's pricing page, its WooPayments fee schedule and its plugin page on WordPress.org.

How do the two models differ?

Shopify is software you rent. You pay a subscription and Shopify runs the store: the pricing page states that every plan includes secure hosting with unlimited bandwidth and a free SSL certificate, and that Shopify is certified Level 1 PCI DSS compliant. You work in one admin, pick a theme and add apps. You do not touch a server.

WooCommerce is software you own and operate. Its WordPress.org page describes it as the open-source ecommerce platform for WordPress, with more than 7 million active installations. The core platform costs nothing, and WooCommerce stresses that open source means you keep full ownership of your store's content and data. In exchange, the store lives on a WordPress site that someone has to host, secure and update. WooCommerce's hosting page puts it plainly: the first step is to choose your host.

Everything else in this comparison follows from that difference.

Two lines are missing from that list because they depend on you: the domain, and the time or the developer needed to build and maintain the site. A store owner who is comfortable with WordPress can keep that line low. A store that needs custom work pays for it on either platform, but on WooCommerce nothing is included by default.

Three differences matter more than the headline rate:

  • Shopify's rate falls as the plan rises: 2.7% + 30¢ on Grow, 2.5% + 30¢ on Advanced and 2.25% + 30¢ on Plus. WooPayments publishes one rate and invites you to contact it about custom pricing.
  • Shopify adds a fee when you use another processor. With a payment provider other than Shopify Payments, Shopify adds a third-party transaction fee of 2%, 1%, 0.6% or 0.2% depending on the plan, on top of that provider's fees. On WooCommerce, you choose any processor and pay its fees alone.
  • WooPayments has its own extras. Its fee schedule adds 1% on any order containing a subscription payment when the subscription uses the Stripe Billing engine, and 1.5% on instant payouts.

If you plan to negotiate rates with a processor of your choice, or to run several gateways side by side, WooCommerce leaves you free to do it. If you are happy with the platform's own processor, Shopify's lower rates on higher plans can outweigh that freedom.

Which is easier to run day to day?

Shopify asks less of you. Hosting, the SSL certificate and automatic updates come with every plan, 24/7 live chat support is included, and the Help Center's setup checklist walks through the steps in order. Shopify notes that the most time-consuming step is usually adding and organizing your products, not the technical setup.

WooCommerce asks you to operate a WordPress site. Its WordPress.org page lists the server requirements (PHP 7.4 or greater, MySQL 5.5.5 or MariaDB 10.1 or greater), and its update guide recommends making a current backup and testing each update on a staging site whenever possible. A managed WooCommerce host takes on part of that work, which is one reason hosting prices vary so widely.

Neither is hard for someone who has done it before. The question is who does it in your business, and what an hour of that person's time is worth.

Which gives you more control?

WooCommerce does, by design. The code is open, so a developer can change any part of the store, the checkout included. WooCommerce states that your store data is yours and that you can export all your content and take your site to any platform you choose.

Shopify trades part of that control for convenience:

  • The checkout: checkout customization is limited on Basic, Grow and Advanced, and full on Plus.
  • The URLs: products and collections live under fixed paths, /products and /collections, that can't be redirected.
  • The catalog: a product can have 2,048 variants at most and three options.
  • The rules: Shopify's Terms of Service allow it to suspend or terminate an account for any reason, without notice and at any time, unless otherwise required by law, and commit it to 30 days' advance notice before any change in fees.

For most stores, those limits never bite. They matter if your business depends on an unusual checkout, a complex catalog or full ownership of the stack. Our Shopify review goes through them one by one.

Apps and extensions

Both platforms rely on add-ons for what the core does not do.

  • Shopify: the Shopify App Store lists over 16,000 apps. Each developer sets its price, and most charges land on your Shopify bill. Our best Shopify apps comparisons sort them by need.
  • WooCommerce: its WordPress.org page mentions hundreds of free and paid extensions in the WooCommerce Marketplace, vetted and reviewed by its developers, in addition to the wider WordPress plugin ecosystem.

Before you choose a platform, check that the tools you rely on exist there. One example from our side: Kanal, the WhatsApp marketing and sales app we build, is a Shopify app, installed from the Shopify App Store.

How do you move from WooCommerce to Shopify?

Shopify publishes a migration guide for WooCommerce stores. It offers three routes:

  1. The Store Migration app, Shopify's own tool, which imports WooCommerce products. It is in early access, available for certain stores and on desktop.
  2. CSV files: you export your data from WooCommerce and import it into Shopify. Shopify warns that some data can't be migrated this way.
  3. Third-party migration apps from the Shopify App Store.

The same guide mentions a shortcut for design: enter your WooCommerce store URL in the theme store section of the admin, and Shopify generates a theme based on your existing storefront.

Whatever the route, three checks protect your sales: redirect every old URL to its new address (Shopify accepts 100,000 redirects per store, more on Plus), tell customers how their accounts carry over, and place test orders before you point the domain to the new store.

Shopify or WooCommerce: which should you choose?

Choose Shopify if:

  • nobody on your team wants to manage hosting, updates and backups;
  • you want one bill and one admin for the store, payments and support;
  • you expect to grow into lower card rates on Grow, Advanced or Plus.

Choose WooCommerce if:

  • you already run WordPress, or have a developer who does;
  • you need to change the checkout, the data model or the code itself;
  • you want to pick your own processor without a platform fee.

Still undecided? List the three things your store must do that a standard store does not. If Shopify and its apps cover them, the hosted route is simpler. If one of them needs custom code at the core of the store, WooCommerce gives you the room. And if the real alternative is a marketplace, our Shopify vs Etsy comparison covers that choice. For other hosted options, see Shopify and BigCommerce, Shopify vs Squarespace and Shopify vs Wix.

In short

Shopify and WooCommerce can cost the same at the start: $29 a month for Shopify Basic paid yearly against $25 a month at the low end of WooCommerce's hosting range, with identical US card rates of 2.9% plus 30 cents. What separates them is who does the work and who sets the rules. Shopify runs the platform and keeps control of it. WooCommerce hands you both the control and the maintenance. Price your real setup on each side, extensions and development included, before you decide.

Frequently asked questions

Is WooCommerce cheaper than Shopify?

It can be, but not by default. The WooCommerce plugin has no monthly subscription, while Shopify Basic costs $39 a month, or $29 paid yearly, in the United States. WooCommerce's own pricing page then counts hosting at $25 to $350 a month for most stores and extensions at $29 to $299 a year each. The cheaper option depends on the hosting, extensions and development time your store needs. Prices read on October 7, 2026.

Is WooCommerce really free?

The core plugin is. WooCommerce describes it as free, open source and without a monthly subscription, platform fees or revenue share. Running a store on it is not free: you pay for WordPress hosting, a domain, any paid extensions or themes, and your payment processor's fees. With WooPayments in the United States, card payments cost 2.9% + $0.30 each as of October 7, 2026.

Which is easier to use, Shopify or WooCommerce?

Shopify asks less of you technically. Hosting, bandwidth and an SSL certificate are included in every plan, and you set up the store from one admin. WooCommerce is a plugin for WordPress: you choose a host, install WordPress and the plugin, and keep them updated. WooCommerce's documentation recommends a backup and a test on a staging site before each update.

Are payment fees lower on WooCommerce or on Shopify?

For US card payments, the entry rates were the same on October 7, 2026: 2.9% + 30¢ online with Shopify Payments on the Basic plan, and 2.9% + $0.30 with WooPayments. The difference appears elsewhere. Shopify's rate falls on higher plans, to 2.5% + 30¢ on Advanced. And if you use an external processor, Shopify adds a third-party transaction fee of 2% on Basic, while WooCommerce charges no platform fee.

Can I migrate from WooCommerce to Shopify?

Yes. Shopify's Help Center publishes a migration guide with three routes: its Store Migration app, in early access for certain stores, which imports WooCommerce products; CSV exports and imports, which do not carry every type of data; and third-party migration apps from the Shopify App Store. Plan the redirects, the customer accounts and a round of test orders before you switch your domain.
