Types of loyalty programs

Before choosing an app, it helps to understand the four main loyalty program models and when each one works best:

  • Points programs. Customers earn points for purchases, account creation, social follows and other actions, then redeem them for discounts or free products. This is the most common model and works well across all store sizes.
  • Tiered programs. Customers unlock higher reward levels such as Silver, Gold and Platinum as they spend more. Tiers create aspirational targets that incentivize larger and more frequent orders.
  • Referral programs. Existing customers share a unique link or code with friends. Both the referrer and the new customer receive a reward. Referral programs rely on a recommendation from a friend, which makes them particularly credible.
  • Cashback programs. Customers receive a percentage of their purchase back as store credit. Cashback is simple to understand and drives repeat purchases with minimal friction.

Most of the apps below support several of these models, so you can combine points with tiers and referrals to create a layered loyalty experience.

What to look for in a loyalty app

The loyalty category is crowded, and the apps look similar at a glance. These criteria separate a good fit from a costly mismatch.

  • Earning and redemption flexibility. You should be able to define exactly how points are earned and what they can be redeemed for, including non-discount rewards such as free shipping or early access, which protect margin better than blanket discounts.
  • On-brand customization. A loyalty widget and emails that match your brand fit your store far better than a generic third-party panel. Check how much you can restyle without custom code.
  • Shopify POS and checkout integration. If you sell in person or want points to apply seamlessly at checkout, confirm the app supports POS and Shopify's checkout extensibility.
  • Analytics and ROI reporting. Look for clear reporting on enrolled members, repeat purchase rate and revenue attributed to the program. Without this you cannot tell whether the program pays for itself.
  • Pricing that scales with you. Many loyalty apps price by order volume or active members. Model your cost at your expected scale, not just today, since jumps between tiers can be steep.
  • Communication and reach. A loyalty program only works if members know their point balance and rewards. Apps that integrate with email, SMS or messaging channels keep members engaged. This is where a dedicated communication tool complements your loyalty app.

The 13 best loyalty apps for Shopify

#1 Smile.io, the gold standard in points and tiers

Smile.io is one of the most established loyalty apps on Shopify, powering a very large number of stores worldwide. It offers a comprehensive out-of-the-box solution covering points, VIP tiers from Bronze through Gold, and a built-in referral program. The visual customization options let you match the loyalty widget to your brand without touching code. The free plan is functional enough for small stores to test the waters, while paid plans start at $15/month (Essential) and the $199/month Growth plan unlocks VIP tiers, points expiry and redemption at checkout on Shopify Plus. Smile.io is the safest bet for merchants who want proven infrastructure with minimal setup and a large partner ecosystem.

#2 LoyaltyLion, enterprise-grade customization

LoyaltyLion is built for mid-market and enterprise brands that need granular control over every aspect of their loyalty program. Its advanced segmentation engine lets you create custom earning rules based on product categories, order frequency and customer attributes. The custom tier system is fully configurable, and the referral engine supports multi-step tracking with fraud detection. A free plan exists for small programs, but the paid plans reflect the feature depth, starting around $199/month. For brands already doing significant revenue, the depth of the tool can justify the investment, and integrations with Klaviyo and Gorgias put loyalty data where the team already works.

#3 Loyoly, gamified missions that drive engagement

Loyoly stands out with its missions-based approach to earning rewards. Rather than simply awarding points per dollar spent, Loyoly lets you create engaging missions: follow the brand on Instagram, leave a review, refer a friend, complete a quiz or share a product on social media. This gamification drives deeper engagement and generates user-generated content as a side benefit. Custom tiers keep high-value customers motivated, and the referral program is tightly integrated. With a free plan and paid plans from $99/month, Loyoly suits brands that want loyalty to double as a community-building engine, and its Kanal integration sends mission and reward updates on WhatsApp.

#4 Yotpo Loyalty, full-suite enterprise loyalty

Yotpo Loyalty, formerly Swell Rewards, is part of the broader Yotpo ecosystem, which includes reviews, SMS marketing and subscriptions. The advantage is a unified data layer: loyalty points, review activity and purchase history all feed into one customer profile, enabling highly targeted reward triggers. VIP tiers are flexible and support both spend-based and action-based progression. The free plan covers basic points and referrals, making it accessible to test, but the real power unlocks at the $199/month tier where advanced segmentation and integrations come into play. It is the strongest choice for stores already using other Yotpo products. Each Yotpo product has its own price list: our guide to what Yotpo costs for reviews and loyalty goes through them.

#5 BON Loyalty, simple and affordable for growing stores

BON Loyalty focuses on doing the fundamentals well at a price point that works for small and growing stores. The points and rewards system covers all the essentials, earn on purchases and redeem for discounts, with basic tier levels to encourage higher spending. The referral program is straightforward and effective, and the widget translates into multiple languages for international stores. Where BON shines is accessibility: the free plan covers up to 150 orders per month, and paid plans start at just $15/month. If you want a clean, no-fuss loyalty program without the complexity or cost of enterprise tools, BON is a smart starting point.

#6 Growave, loyalty, reviews and wishlists in one app

Growave bundles loyalty, reviews, wishlists and social sharing into a single app, reducing the need to install and manage several tools. The all-in-one points system rewards purchases, reviews, birthdays and social engagement. Tiers incentivize repeat buying, and the social sharing feature turns loyalty actions into organic reach on Instagram and Facebook. The free plan covers basics for small catalogs, with paid plans from $15/month. Growave is ideal for merchants who want to consolidate their tech stack and prefer one dashboard over four separate apps, and consolidating also keeps the storefront lighter than running each tool separately.

#7 Rivo Loyalty, fast, modern and founder-friendly

Rivo Loyalty has built a strong reputation among DTC founders for its modern interface, fast setup and responsive support. It covers points, VIP tiers and referrals with a clean widget that is easy to customize to your brand. Rivo leans into the needs of growing brands rather than enterprises, and its developer-friendly approach makes deeper customization accessible for stores with a designer or developer on hand. The free plan lets small stores launch a program quickly, with paid plans from $15/month as you scale. It is a strong choice for ambitious DTC brands that want a contemporary alternative to the longest-standing players.

#8 CWILL (formerly Loloyal), feature-rich value for cost-conscious stores

CWILL, formerly Loloyal, packs a lot into an affordable package: points, VIP tiers, referrals and reward campaigns all sit under one widget. It includes a points expiry system, custom branding and a content editor that lets you restyle the loyalty page without code. CWILL also offers data migration from other loyalty apps, which lowers the switching cost if you are leaving a pricier platform, and it supports Shopify POS. With a usable free plan and paid plans from $19/month, it is a sensible pick for stores that want a broad feature set without an enterprise budget.

#9 Appstle Loyalty, affordable points, credits and referrals

Appstle Loyalty comes from the team behind Appstle Subscriptions, and it brings the same focus on responsive support and frequent updates. The program covers points, store credit, VIP tiers and referrals, with rules you can configure per product or collection. Store credit as a redemption option is a useful margin-friendly alternative to blanket discounts. A free plan is available and paid plans start at just $10/month, making Appstle one of the most accessible entry points for stores that want loyalty and subscriptions managed by the same vendor, with subscribers earning points on every renewal.

#10 Yuko Loyalty, all-in-one loyalty, reviews and referrals

Yuko combines loyalty, reviews and referrals in one Shopify dashboard, with paid plans starting at just $12/month and a free tier for new stores. Built by the team behind WPLoyalty, a loyalty solution for WooCommerce, Yuko brings a proven product playbook to Shopify. Stores get points, VIP tiers, 15+ ways to earn points, referrals and photo and video reviews in a single workflow without syncing multiple apps. The referral engine includes two-sided rewards and anti-fraud protection, while migration support is available for Smile, Yotpo, Judge.me, Loox and Stamped users. It is an affordable, all-in-one retention solution for growing Shopify brands.

#11 Marsello, loyalty plus built-in marketing automation

Marsello combines a loyalty program with email and SMS marketing automation in a single platform. Points, VIP tiers and referrals work alongside automated campaigns triggered by customer behavior, so you can reward members and re-engage them from the same dashboard. It also connects to Shopify POS, making it a strong fit for retailers selling both online and in store, with a unified customer view across both. Marsello sits at a higher price point, from $60/month with no free plan, which reflects the combined loyalty and marketing scope rather than loyalty alone.

#12 Gameball, gamified loyalty that drives engagement

Gameball leans into gamification, layering challenges, badges, levels and a spin-the-wheel mechanic on top of a standard points and VIP tier program. The game-like elements give customers more reasons to return between purchases, which can lift engagement for brands with frequent buying cycles. Referrals are built in, the widget is customizable to your brand, and segmentation lets you target challenges at specific groups. With a free plan and paid plans from $34/month, Gameball suits stores that want loyalty to feel interactive rather than transactional.

#13 Joy Loyalty, a polished program at an accessible price

Joy Loyalty packs points, VIP tiers, paid memberships and referrals into an app that stays affordable as your store grows. Its strength is breadth of touchpoints: its listing counts more than 30 moments in the shopper journey where rewards surface, from a branded loyalty page to on-site widgets and post-purchase prompts. The app is built for Shopify and Shopify Plus, supports POS, and has a generous free plan with paid tiers from $29/month. Joy is a strong fit for merchants who want a complete, on-brand loyalty experience without an enterprise budget.

How to choose based on store size

Picking the right loyalty app depends largely on where your store sits today and where it is headed:

  • Just starting out, under $50K per month. Start with BON Loyalty or Smile.io on their free plans. Both let you validate that your customers respond to a points program before you invest in paid features.
  • Growing stores, $50K to $500K per month. At this stage, gamification and referral programs start to move the needle. Loyoly, Rivo Loyalty and Growave offer strong mid-range feature sets at reasonable price points.
  • Established brands, $500K and above per month. Invest in LoyaltyLion or Yotpo Loyalty for enterprise-level segmentation, analytics and custom tier logic. The deeper your customer data, the more precisely you can reward high-value segments and reduce churn at scale.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best loyalty app for Shopify?

Smile.io, #1 in our ranking, is the safest all-round choice for most stores thanks to proven infrastructure and easy setup, with Rivo and CWILL (formerly Loloyal) as modern alternatives at similar entry prices. Enterprise brands should look at LoyaltyLion or Yotpo Loyalty for segmentation depth, and stores that want missions and user-generated content will prefer Loyoly. Whichever you choose, pair it with a channel members actually read; Kanal handles that on WhatsApp.

Are there free loyalty apps for Shopify?

Yes. Smile.io, LoyaltyLion, Loyoly, Yotpo Loyalty, BON, Growave, Rivo, CWILL, Appstle, Yuko, Gameball and Joy all offer free plans, usually capped by monthly orders or limited to basic points and referrals. Free plans are enough to validate that your customers respond to rewards; VIP tiers, custom branding and analytics typically sit behind paid tiers from $10 to $34 a month.

Do loyalty programs actually work for small Shopify stores?

They can, as long as they stay simple. The key is to keep the earning and redemption mechanics easy to understand. Start with a basic structure such as "earn 1 point per dollar, redeem 100 points for $5 off" and refine from there based on customer behavior, tracking the repeat purchase rate of members against that of other customers.

How long does it take to see results from a loyalty program?

There is no published benchmark: it depends on how often your customers buy. The first month is typically a ramp-up period as customers discover and enroll in the program. Then compare the repeat purchase rate of members with that of non-members over the same periods, and wait at least one full purchase cycle before you optimize point values, tier thresholds and reward types.

Can I run a loyalty program alongside discount codes and sales?

Yes, and you should. Loyalty programs and promotional discounts serve different purposes. Discounts drive short-term urgency, while loyalty programs build long-term retention. The best practice is to let customers earn points even on discounted orders so they feel rewarded regardless of how they bought. Just set a minimum order value if margin is a concern.

Should I combine multiple loyalty apps?

No. Running two competing points programs confuses customers and can create conflicting discount logic. Pick one dedicated loyalty app. If you want members to stay engaged, pair that single loyalty app with a communication channel such as Kanal rather than a second loyalty app.

Do loyalty apps work with Shopify POS and Shopify Plus?

Most established loyalty apps support Shopify POS so customers earn and redeem points in store, including Smile.io, LoyaltyLion, Yotpo, CWILL, Marsello and Joy. Shopify Plus stores should check for checkout extensibility support so points can be redeemed inside checkout without a discount code, which the larger platforms generally offer. Confirm both before choosing if you sell in person or on Plus.
