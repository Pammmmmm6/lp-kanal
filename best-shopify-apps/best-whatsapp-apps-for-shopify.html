Chat button or full platform: two kinds of WhatsApp app

Most confusion in this category comes from two very different products sharing a name. A chat widget (SeedGrow, Chaty) adds a WhatsApp button to your storefront; when a visitor taps it, a conversation opens in their WhatsApp app with your regular business number. It is cheap and quick to install, and the conversation always starts with the visitor: a widget does not send reminders or campaigns.

A WhatsApp Business API platform (Kanal, Zoko, Wati, Dondy, Interakt, TextYess, SleekFlow, Charles) connects an official API number so you can send approved template messages to customers who opted in, build flows triggered by Shopify events, run broadcasts, share catalogs and let an AI reply. It costs a subscription plus Meta's messaging fees, and it is the route to abandoned cart recovery, campaigns and order notifications on WhatsApp. If you only want a contact button, a widget is enough. If you want revenue from the channel, you need the API. Pushdaddy sits between the two: a chat widget and live chat that can also send automated cart, order and tracking messages from your own number.

What to look for in a WhatsApp app for Shopify

  • Official API and Meta verification. The app should register your number on the WhatsApp Business API, handle template approval and give you a verified business profile. Without it, there is no automation.
  • Shopify data depth. Products, customers, orders and abandoned checkouts should sync automatically so messages carry cart contents, tracking links and the right language and currency.
  • Flow builder and triggers. Look for flows triggered by Shopify events (checkout abandoned, order fulfilled, delivery confirmed) with delays, conditions and suppression once the customer converts.
  • AI reply quality. Test how the AI answers a product question, a delivery question and an out-of-scope request, and how cleanly it hands off to a person.
  • Opt-in and compliance. GDPR consent capture, opt-out handling, and respect for Meta's 24-hour service window and template rules should be built in, not left to you.
  • Pricing transparency. Compare the subscription, the number of included agent seats and contacts, and Meta's per-message fees at your expected volume.
  • Integrations. Klaviyo, Gorgias, Zendesk and subscription platforms decide whether WhatsApp joins your stack or sits beside it.

You want carts recovered and campaigns sent automatically

Four questions that decide the choice

The checklist earlier in this guide describes what a good WhatsApp app looks like. These four questions decide which one to install on your store.

1. Is cart recovery your priority?

The Baymard Institute puts the average documented online cart abandonment rate at 70.22% (page read on October 7, 2026), which is why recovery is usually the first automation merchants switch on. It takes an API platform to send that reminder, because a widget never messages first. Among the platforms here, Kanal, Zoko, Dondy, Interakt, Wati and TextYess all connect to Shopify checkout events. Our abandoned cart recovery strategies guide covers timing and sequences.

2. How much of the conversation can you automate?

Manual replies get harder to sustain as conversations add up. Kanal's AI chatbot answers product, order and delivery questions and hands off to a person when a request goes beyond them. Several other API platforms here ship AI replies too (TextYess, Zoko, Dondy, SleekFlow, Wati, Interakt); with a widget, every message is answered by a person. Before you commit, test each app on a question outside its training data and watch how the handoff works. Our WhatsApp chatbot for e-commerce guide shows what the automation covers in practice.

3. Does it fit the stack you already run?

WhatsApp should join your existing tools rather than sit beside them. Kanal integrates with Klaviyo, Gorgias, Zendesk, Crisp and Recharge, so flows, support tickets and subscription events work together. Apply the same test to any other app: an inbox that cannot see the helpdesk ticket, or a campaign tool that cannot read your email segments, is a second silo to maintain. If Klaviyo is the tool in question, our Klaviyo pricing guide explains how Klaviyo bills its own SMS and WhatsApp messages.

4. Price, or return on the price?

Do not choose on the headline subscription alone. Count what the app automates (cart reminders, order updates, campaigns) against its plan price, the per-conversation fees some vendors add, and Meta's per-message fees, which apply on top of every API subscription. Compare the all-in cost of your messages at your expected volume rather than the plan price.

No single channel wins on every metric. Email is the lowest-cost channel and your list is an owned asset. SMS has near-universal reach but plain text and per-message carrier fees. WhatsApp adds two-way conversations, rich media and product catalogs, in the messaging app customers already use in the markets where it dominates.

How much do WhatsApp messages cost?

Meta charges per delivered message, with rates that vary by the customer's country and by category (marketing, utility, authentication, service). Since October 1, 2026, Meta gives each business phone number 1,000 service messages per month at no charge and bills from the 1,001st, and messages in a free entry point window are not charged (see Meta's pricing page, read on October 7, 2026). API apps add these fees to their subscription, so compare the subscription plus your expected message volume.

WhatsApp is widely used in most of Europe, Latin America, India, the Middle East and Africa, which makes it a natural channel for stores selling across markets. Message templates are approved per language, and Meta's rates depend on the country of the customer who receives the message. Check that your app supports multiple template languages and, if you need them, separate sender numbers per market.
